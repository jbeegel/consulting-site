## Key Question 1: How does collegiate licensing work mechanically? (agencies, application/renewal, license types, artwork approval portals, labels/holograms, labor-compliance)

### Takeaway
A multi-school licensee operates inside a fragmented, agency-mediated system: CLC/Learfield (the dominant agency, ~120+ staff, portal = Brand Manager 360 + JPatton "Logos on Demand" for artwork/holograms), Affinity Licensing (portal = Affinity Gateway), Fanatics Licensing Management (ex-Fermata), Exemplar Associates ("Workspace" portal), BrandComply/Octane5 (used by self-managed schools like UMass, Ohio State, Iowa, Indiana), CORE81, Nexus, and direct-licensing schools. Every school sets its own rules even within the same agency; every design must be approved per school before production; every retail unit must carry an OLCP hologram/hangtag; licenses run July 1–June 30 and renew annually; and supply-chain disclosure/FLA affiliation is a condition of licensure at many schools.

**License types and application process (CLC)**
- CLC offers two categories: Retail ("sale at retail in approved retail channels and direct to consumer, as well as university departments") and Internal (university departments only; "cannot provide product at retailer direct to consumer"). Hot-market programs (NCAA tournaments, CFP, bowls, conferences) are a separate process applied for via Brand Manager 360. — [CLC Get Licensed](https://clc.com/get-licensed)
- CLC's 10-step process: create account → online application (+ product samples) → application fee → CLC review → ship quality samples → CLC sends to institution → institution decides → if approved: commercial insurance ($1M+), disclose supply-chain manufacturers, register with FLA if required, order hologram labels/hangtags → remit advance fees → sign agreement → "License is Active – Submit Artwork for Approval and Manufacture Product." — [CLC Get Licensed](https://clc.com/get-licensed)
- CLC application fees: $250 non-refundable + $125 per additional school (same for Internal and Retail); "Additional institution-specific fees may apply." Selection criteria include "Dedicated staff to manage the day-to-day licensing process and supply chain compliance" and "Internal infrastructure and systems to manage approval and royalty reporting processes." "Several institutions have pre-existing exclusive agreements in certain product categories and distribution channels that preclude them from considering other vendors." — [CLC Get Licensed](https://clc.com/get-licensed)
- Quality samples: Retail applicants must submit at least one generic sample per product type showing the decoration method; some institutions require more. — [CLC Get Licensed](https://clc.com/get-licensed)
- Insurance: $2M aggregate / $1M per occurrence incl. product liability for all CLC licensees; higher for consumables, UL items, health & beauty, infant non-apparel, candles; "all product liability insurance must be written exactly as shown... No deviations will be accepted"; a license agreement is not sent until insurance is in place. — [CLC Get Licensed](https://clc.com/get-licensed)
- Local vs Standard license (CLC): a Local/Crafter's license is for sellers under ~2,000 units/year, with royalties paid upfront (min ~$250) and sales limited to local channels; Standard is for larger programs paying quarterly per-unit royalties. — [Collegiate Licensing World, "What is a CLC Local License?"](https://www.collegiatelicensingworld.com/what-is-a-clc-local-license)
- A small Ohio decorator's first-hand account (older, ~2015): CLC's Phase I application guide was 37 pages; fees were $500 standard/$100 local at the time; Phase II required design submissions, hologram orders, insurance certificate naming CLC, product spec sheets, and access to "myiclc" and Logos On Demand; Phase III = final approval, advance royalties per university, signatures. "The CLC has the most challenging application process I've ever undergone." Comments on the post as recent as March 2026 ask "Is there anyone that you can get to fill out all the license paperwork for you." — [Jupmode blog](https://www.jupmode.com/blogs/news/collegiate-licensing-group-application-how-to)
- Timeline: Ithaca says 4–10 weeks to become licensed depending on type; Nebraska says application timelines are 4–15 weeks and application fees $100–$1,000; a 2026 industry guide estimates agency review 2–4 weeks + university review 4–12 weeks ("2–4 months on average") and advises planning "6–9 months from first contact with the school to first shipment." LSU/CLC: "we cannot accommodate 'rush' requests." — [Ithaca](https://www.ithaca.edu/marketing-communications/licensing-and-trademark-enforcement/licensing-and-approval-process); [Nebraska licensing guidelines](https://licensing.unl.edu/how-become-licensed/guidelinescriteria-obtaining-license/); [CLW 2026 guide](https://www.collegiatelicensingworld.com/how-you-can-get-an-officially-licensed-collegiate-product-to-market-in-2026-step-by-step-guide); [LSU FAQ](https://lsu.edu/trademark/getting-licensed/faq.php)
- Nebraska's published cost stack for a new licensee: admin fees $100–1,000; holograms $50+; liability insurance $200–4,000; Logos on Demand $50–500; FLA $100–10,000; advance fees $1,000 (apparel) / $500 (non-apparel); standard royalty "16% but varies depending on product category." Nebraska also lists nine exclusive programs (mid-tier/better apparel, headwear, jerseys, loungewear, mass, performance apparel) that lock out other applicants. — [Nebraska licensing guidelines](https://licensing.unl.edu/how-become-licensed/guidelinescriteria-obtaining-license/)
- Logos on Demand (JPatton) subscription pricing per Nebraska: $50/institution for 1–5 brands, sliding to $25/institution at 150+ brands, capped at $5,000. — [Nebraska licensing guidelines](https://licensing.unl.edu/how-become-licensed/guidelinescriteria-obtaining-license/)
- Annual term: all CSUSB/Learfield agreements expire June 30; renewal admin fees scale with school count (1 school $200; 2–5 schools $500; 6+ = $150 per institution). Learfield applications state terms run July 1–June 30 and licensees "will be given the option to renew in May/June." — [CSUSB](https://www.csusb.edu/marketing-communications/csusb-identity-brand/trademarks-promotional-items/licensing-agreements); [Tarleton/Learfield application PDF](https://www.tarleton.edu/licensing/wp-content/uploads/sites/109/2022/07/Learfield-Licensing-Partners-License-Application.pdf)
- Affinity application steps (APSU example): create Affinity Gateway account → select school, enter business history and categories → $200 non-refundable application fee per client via ACH/card → upload COI ($1M GL, Affinity and clients as additional insured) → mail physical samples → e-sign agreement. "Once licensed, all product designs must be submitted via the Affinity Gateway for approval prior to production. Sales are reported quarterly for both royalty-exempt and royalty-bearing items." Standard royalty 12% of gross sales; $50 minimum annual royalty. Affinity licenses renew annually on July 1. — [APSU Vendor Hub](https://www.apsu.edu/govup/vendor-hub.php); [Augusta Affinity process PDF (via search snippet)](https://web2.augusta.edu/dcm/marketing/affinity-licensing-app-process.pdf)

**Artwork approval workflow**
- Brand Manager 360 is CLC's "online license management tool for product and design approval"; a Learfield job posting describes it as handling "submitting artwork, reporting royalties, disclosing suppliers and all aspects of license management." Ole Miss: licensee shares brand-approved artwork in Brand360; the licensing department "will approve, decline, or request edits"; production starts only after approval. — [Ole Miss licensed products page](https://olemiss.edu/marcomm/creative-services/brand-services/promotional_materials_and_licensed_products); [CSUSB licensing process](https://www.csusb.edu/marketing-communications/csusb-identity-brand/trademarks-promotional-items/licensing-process)
- UT Austin requires art review "prior to production" even for internal giveaways and notes some universities use an "Internal Request Portal" instead of BM360. — [UT Austin FAQ PDF](https://trademarks.utexas.edu/sites/default/files/Frequently%20Asked%20Questions%202.pdf)
- Reviewers check "the exact logo file, color values, clear space, trademark symbols, wording, co-branding, and placement"; "A mark copied from an old web page may look correct to a designer and still fail because the university has replaced it." Typical rejection categories: mark integrity (distorted/stretched/incorrect PMS colors), clear space & sizing, restricted marks (presidential seals, commemorative marks), co-branding/third-party marks/NIL. "The original design file must be kept, and any changes require reapproval." — [Licensing International, Aug 2026](https://licensinginternational.org/news/what-brands-need-to-get-right-in-collegiate-merchandise/); [Licensing International, Nov 2026](https://licensinginternational.org/news/what-to-do-after-getting-a-collegiate-license/)
- Color compliance: SMU specifies Pantone 186 C and says non-brand colors "require special approval"; Tulane acknowledges some pre-set vendor color options can't take custom Pantone numbers and tells buyers to pick the closest shade. — [SMU licensing](https://smumustangs.com/sports/2016/6/8/ot-smu-licensing-html); [Tulane merchandise FAQ](https://communications.tulane.edu/campus-partner-merchandise-faq)
- Turnaround examples: Ithaca ~5 business days; Sacramento State process notes 1–2 business days for CLC-stage review; Utah Tech sends an email explaining denial and either allows corrective changes or requires restarting the process. — [Ithaca](https://www.ithaca.edu/marketing-communications/licensing-and-trademark-enforcement/licensing-and-approval-process); [Sac State approval PDF](https://www.csus.edu/student-affairs/business-operations/_internal/_documents/approval-process-for-branding-of-promotional-items-apparel-and-uniforms.pdf); [Utah Tech](https://licensing.utahtech.edu/campus-licensing/)

- Co-branding: Oregon defines "Co-Brand" products as requiring "specific approval" and "additional licensing terms," and offers specific co-brand license types; manufacturer branding "cannot appear on the exterior of a garment unless it is a Nike product." Arizona: only licensed vendors can access the approval portal. — [UO licensing definitions](https://communications.uoregon.edu/licensing-definitions); [UO manufacturer branding page](https://communications.uoregon.edu/node/225); [BGSU sponsorships & co-branding](https://www.bgsu.edu/marketing-and-communications/bgsu-graphic-standards-manual/university-licensing/sponsorships-and-co-branding.html); [USU brand standards](https://www.usu.edu/brand/standards/perceived-endorsement/index.php); [UT Austin corporate sponsors](https://trademarks.utexas.edu/node/6); [Arizona licensed vendors](https://licensing.arizona.edu/node/4)

**Labels / holograms**
- All CLC retail licensees must order "Officially Licensed Collegiate Products" (OLCP) hologram labels and affix them to all licensed items; CLC also requires a company-name identifier (hangtag, adhesive, sewn-in, printed/etched, or added to the hologram hangtag). — [CLC Get Licensed](https://clc.com/get-licensed)
- The OLCP label "is affixed to more than 100 million collegiate products in the U.S." each year; LSU requires an OLCP hangtag or sticker on every licensed product. — [LSU Look for the Label](https://lsu.edu/trademark/lsu-products/label.php)
- Holograms are purchased from J. Patton's Logos on Demand and ship once the license agreement is fully executed; hot-market events may require special stickers; non-hangtag apparel licensees must affix the hologram to their own hangtag or the outside front chest, not the neck label/inside neck/sleeves/back (vendor order form, possibly dated). — [Nebraska guidelines](https://licensing.unl.edu/how-become-licensed/guidelinescriteria-obtaining-license/); [Wartburg CLC label policy PDF (via search)](https://info.wartburg.edu/Portals/0/Public/clclabelpolicy.pdf)
- Learfield-side schools instead use "Learfield OLP Hangtags" ($64–$100/yr at CSUSB); Oregon (FLM) uses a "GCP" label; Ithaca requires the OLCP label "on the item or packaging." — [CSUSB](https://www.csusb.edu/marketing-communications/csusb-identity-brand/trademarks-promotional-items/licensing-agreements); [UO](https://communications.uoregon.edu/licensing-definitions); [Ithaca](https://www.ithaca.edu/marketing-communications/licensing-and-trademark-enforcement/licensing-and-approval-process)

**Corporate responsibility / supply chain**
- CLC: "your company is responsible for the action or in-action of all manufacturers and suppliers throughout its supply chain"; licensees must disclose their supply chain (manufacturers, finishers, materials providers, locations) and keep disclosures current; many institutions require affiliation with the Fair Labor Association, Worker Rights Consortium, or the Bangladesh Accord, and may require signing university-specific Labor Codes of Conduct. — [CLC Get Licensed](https://clc.com/get-licensed)
- Nebraska requires "Authorized Manufacturers Agreements" (AMA) signed by licensee and each factory/finisher before CLC executes them, full disclosure of factory sites, FLA registration for FLA-affiliated institutions, and a "Special Agreement regarding Labor Codes of Conduct." Example given: a headwear licensee that only applies logos must register the hat manufacturer as the Authorized Manufacturer. — [Nebraska guidelines](https://licensing.unl.edu/how-become-licensed/guidelinescriteria-obtaining-license/)
- Ongoing compliance checklist for licensees: COIs must be renewed before expiry per program; FLA/WRC codes; renewal dates vary (auto-renew vs re-application with updated financials/fees). — [Licensing International, Nov 2026](https://licensinginternational.org/news/what-to-do-after-getting-a-collegiate-license/)

### Inferences
- For a licensee with hundreds of schools, the "system of record" problem is structural: at least four distinct portal families (BM360/Logos on Demand, Affinity Gateway, BrandComply College, Exemplar Workspace/FLM) plus legacy Trademarx/Approvals-on-Demand paths, each with its own artwork library, approval queue, royalty template and renewal calendar. Agency switches (e.g., Houston → CLC in 2026) force re-onboarding.
- The approval workflow is a per-SKU-per-school gate; a design reused across N schools generates N submissions and N potential rejections for reasons that are largely mechanical (wrong logo version, PMS mismatch, clear space, restricted mark, missing TM symbol) — a strong candidate for pre-submission automated checking.
- Per-school exclusives (Nebraska lists nine) mean a licensee's category rights differ school by school; a product that is fine for 300 schools may be prohibited at 50, which has to be checked at the design/listing stage.

## Key Question 2: Royalty reporting — rates, advances/minimums, how reports are compiled, audits, late fees, error-proneness

### Takeaway
Royalties are typically 10–15% of wholesale/net sales (ranging from ~6% to 16–20% for exclusives), reported quarterly within 30 days of quarter end, with zero-sales reports required, advances/minimum guarantees per school, and transaction-level detail expected by some agencies. Each licensor/agency has its own template and portal; the math is applied per line item (school × category × channel × rate), which is why trade and vendor sources consistently describe manual spreadsheet-based reporting as the dominant audit risk.

### Cited Findings
- Royalty rate ranges: 2026 industry guide says "typically 12–15% of wholesale in 2026" and warns applicants to budget "15–18% of your wholesale cost"; Collegiate Licensing World maintains a historical school-by-school rate index bucketed at 13%+, 12%, 10%, and 6–8.5%. — [CLW 2026 guide](https://www.collegiatelicensingworld.com/how-you-can-get-an-officially-licensed-collegiate-product-to-market-in-2026-step-by-step-guide); [CLW royalty rates index](https://www.collegiatelicensingworld.com/collegiate-licensing-school-royalty-rates)
- School examples: CSUSB 12% (0% internal); Idaho 14%; Liberty 14%; Texas Southern 10% (raised to 12% in 2017); Lamar 8% of wholesale; Nebraska 16% standard; Ithaca 10%; UW–Madison typical 12%; Tennessee 10% under standard CLC agreement; Kansas bases royalty on 50% of retail when the manufacturer is also the retailer. — [CSUSB](https://www.csusb.edu/marketing-communications/csusb-identity-brand/trademarks-promotional-items/licensing-agreements); [Idaho](https://uidaho.edu/brand/trademark-licensing/policy-guidelines/licensing-royalties); [Liberty](https://www.liberty.edu/marketing/licensing-branding/registered-liberty-licensees/); [TSU](https://tsu.edu/about/administration/general-counsel/trademarks-and-licensing/licensing.php); [Lamar](https://secure-resources.lamar.edu/marketing-communications/brand-guidelines/licensing-trademarks/licensing/licensing-agent.html); [Nebraska](https://licensing.unl.edu/how-become-licensed/guidelinescriteria-obtaining-license/); [Ithaca](https://www.ithaca.edu/marketing-communications/licensing-and-trademark-enforcement/licensing-and-approval-process); [UW–Madison](https://licensing.wisc.edu/about/introduction-to-trademark-licensing/); [Tennessee](https://tennessee.edu/about/divisions/communications/licensing/licensing-agreements/); [Kansas policy](https://services.ku.edu/TDClient/818/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=21420)
- Exclusive deals push rates to 18–20% (jewelry/watches) with high minimum guarantees and 5-year terms vs 2–3 years for non-exclusive; Ohio State's program generated ~$15M/yr (2017). — [Licensing International, Sept 2017](https://licensinginternational.org/news/colleges-licensees-seek-to-maximize-the-opportunity-via-exclusivity/)
- Royalty base: "typically calculated against net sales... the price you invoice at the first level of distribution"; deductions (freight, returns, discounts) are capped "often somewhere in the 5–10% range of the gross"; distributor sales often carry a higher rate; vintage/heritage and hot-market product can carry separate rate structures; NCAA school rates "typically in the 8–12% range" (vendor blog, Sept 2026). — [RepSpark, "Licensed Apparel 101"](https://www.repspark.com/blog/licensed-apparel-101-how-royalty-calculations-work-in-wholesale-orders)
- Oregon defines Net Sales as gross sales minus approved deductions (returns, allowances, freight) and notes DTC and made-to-order sales "are subject to the same royalty and compliance requirements as other sales channels." — [UO licensing definitions](https://communications.uoregon.edu/licensing-definitions)
- Advances / minimums: UMass $50 annual licensee fee, $200 royalty advance, $300 vintage royalty advance; Nebraska $1,000 apparel / $500 non-apparel advance; Liberty $50–$100 depending on categories; Tennessee "royalty advances dependent on product category"; Maine's standard agreement describes a non-refundable guaranteed minimum credited against earned royalties; Affinity $50 minimum annual royalty per client; CSUSB $0 advance. — [UMass](https://www.massachusetts.edu/trademark-licensing/becoming-licensed/pricing-royalty-reporting); [Nebraska](https://licensing.unl.edu/how-become-licensed/guidelinescriteria-obtaining-license/); [Liberty](https://www.liberty.edu/marketing/licensing-branding/registered-liberty-licensees/); [Tennessee](https://tennessee.edu/about/divisions/communications/licensing/licensing-agreements/); [Maine standard agreement PDF](https://www.maine.edu/general-counsel/wp-content/uploads/sites/49/2019/12/TrademarkLicenseAgreement.Standard.pdf); [APSU/Affinity](https://www.apsu.edu/govup/vendor-hub.php); [CSUSB](https://www.csusb.edu/marketing-communications/csusb-identity-brand/trademarks-promotional-items/licensing-agreements)
- GMR mechanics (general licensed sports apparel): GMR "usually set at roughly half of projected royalties"; licensee pays the higher of GMR or earned royalty, so "royalty exposure isn't just a per-order calculation — it's a running total your team needs visibility into throughout the period." — [RepSpark](https://www.repspark.com/blog/licensed-apparel-101-how-royalty-calculations-work-in-wholesale-orders)
- Cadence/deadlines: UMass requires quarterly reports "regardless of zero sales" due Apr 30 / Jul 31 / Oct 31 / Jan 31 (via BrandComply College); USC: royalty reports and payments due within 30 days of calendar quarter end (via Trademarx); Affinity: quarterly within 30 days of Sep 30/Dec 31/Mar 31/Jun 30 with "per transaction detail of sales containing our client's marks" (customer or university name, payment date, invoice number, product description, quantity, unit price, gross sales), submitted via Affinity's PDF/Excel template, own spreadsheet, or QuickBooks export; Affinity's accounting team "examine[s] each report to ensure royalties are calculated correctly, a report for each organization is submitted, and all required detail is represented." — [UMass](https://www.massachusetts.edu/trademark-licensing/becoming-licensed/pricing-royalty-reporting); [USC](https://trademarks.usc.edu/?p=56); [Affinity/greeklicensing royalty tips (2016)](https://greeklicensing.com/blog/2016/07/48/quarterly-royalty-reporting-tips-for-success); [APSU](https://www.apsu.edu/govup/vendor-hub.php)
- Nebraska expects licensees to "Maintain monthly reports on sales, royalty, etc." and to "Use IMG College Licensing systems throughout the term." — [Nebraska](https://licensing.unl.edu/how-become-licensed/guidelinescriteria-obtaining-license/)
- Trade guidance (Nov 2026): "Collegiate royalty reporting requires accurate sales data for each SKU, not just end-of-quarter bookkeeping... Manually tracking sales across multiple portals and spreadsheets increases the risk of late reports, inconsistent records, and audit errors." One university reportedly will not accept royalty reports unless each order is linked to approved artwork. — [Licensing International, Nov 2026](https://licensinginternational.org/news/what-to-do-after-getting-a-collegiate-license/); [search summary citing a university rule](https://www.collegiatelicensingworld.com/brandcomply)
- Audit exposure (general licensing, not collegiate-specific): licensors hold audit rights; if underpayment exceeds a threshold (commonly 2–5%, sometimes 5–10%) the licensee pays audit costs; one study found 60% of agreements examined had underreported sales; a vendor-cited 2025 IAM report says 75–95% of royalty audits find discrepancies. — [RepSpark](https://www.repspark.com/blog/licensed-apparel-101-how-royalty-calculations-work-in-wholesale-orders); [beancount royalty audit guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/29/royalty-audit-licensee-underpayment-small-licensor-guide); [FADEL](https://fadel.com/resources/the-hidden-cost-of-royalty-processing/)
- Octane5 markets consumer-engagement/authentication tools for "identifying royalty under-reporting and other compliance issues." — [Licensing International on Octane5/Brandr](https://licensinginternational.org/news/octane5-enters-the-nil-licensing-game-with-the-brandr-group/)
- SKU is explicitly defined in licensor vocab as the key for royalty reporting: "In licensed product reporting, SKUs help the university and its partners monitor which products are being sold and in what quantities." — [UO licensing definitions](https://communications.uoregon.edu/licensing-definitions)

### Inferences
- A licensee with ~300+ schools across 3–4 agencies files on the order of 1,200+ school-quarter reports a year, each requiring the same underlying sales ledger to be re-cut by school, category, channel (wholesale vs DTC vs distributor), and rate — and in some cases to Affinity's transaction-level format. This is the single most mechanical, high-volume, high-penalty workflow in the business.
- Because rates, advances, deductions caps, and zero-report rules differ by school and change annually (July 1 renewals), a rules engine keyed on school × category × channel × effective date is the prerequisite for automating royalty compilation.
- Advances/minimums across hundreds of small schools create a long-tail drag: $200–$1,000 per school per year in advances, plus $150–$200 per-school renewal fees, plus hologram/Logos-on-Demand subscriptions, regardless of whether that school sells.

### Gaps
- No public documentation of late-fee/interest amounts in CLC or Affinity agreements was found.
- No collegiate-specific audit statistics (frequency of CLC/Affinity audits, typical findings) were found; audit figures above are from general licensing sources.
- Could not retrieve the actual BM360 royalty submission format (whether it requires per-SKU, per-school summary, or file upload).

## Key Question 3: Standard complaints / pain points of licensees, and the licensing-software vendor landscape

### Takeaway
Licensee-side complaints that recur across trade press, university pages, vendor marketing and the one first-hand decorator account found: the application is heavy and slow; every design is re-approved per school; approval requirements differ per school even within one agency; marks and style guides go stale; co-branding/NIL adds another rights layer; renewals, COIs and factory disclosures must be tracked across multiple portals; royalty reporting is spreadsheet-driven and error-prone; and exclusives/consolidation shrink the addressable school list. Software exists (Octane5 BrandComply, Dependable Solutions, FADEL, MyMediabox, Vistex, RepSpark) but is licensor-side or generic; no collegiate-licensee-side multi-agency aggregation tool was found.

### Cited Findings
- "One university has retired the logo used in the artwork. Another has an exclusive partner in that product category. A third allows the mark, but not the slogan placed beside it. The retailer wants a quick launch, yet the license only covers a different sales channel... each educational institution sets its own rules, even when several schools work with the same licensing agency." Recommended remedy: treat every product as a record linking "institution, license, product category, approved artwork, manufacturer, SKU, sales channel, and approval date." — [Licensing International, Aug 2026](https://licensinginternational.org/news/what-brands-need-to-get-right-in-collegiate-merchandise/)
- "Which homecoming hoodie design did University B approve? Did the embroidery shop use the approved file or the initial design? - FADEL (LicenSee) and MyMediabox market licensee-side royalty/product-approval software; FADEL notes "many licensors require that licensees follow their own proprietary templates for royalty reporting, and each has its own quirks." — [FADEL](https://fadel.com/resources/the-hidden-cost-of-royalty-processing/); [MyMediabox product approval](https://www.mymediabox.com/mbox/)

### Inferences
- The tooling market is licensor-centric (BrandComply, BM360, Gateway, Trademarx are built for the agency/school). The licensee is the integration point that has to normalize across all of them; this is the whitespace for custom AI/automation (artwork pre-flight per school style guide, multi-portal submission tracking, royalty ledger normalization, COI/renewal calendar, exclusivity/category rights check at quoting time).
- Because agencies explicitly rank "internal infrastructure and systems to manage approval and royalty reporting" as a licensing criterion, better internal tooling is also a sales/renewal argument with schools, not just a cost saver.

## Key Question 5: Campus store channel — Follett, BNC/BNED, Slingshot, independents; how buyers buy; vendor complaints

### Takeaway
The campus-store channel is dominated by two operators (Follett: 1,200+ stores and 1,800 e-commerce sites; BNED/Barnes & Noble College: ~800 stores) plus Slingshot and independents (NACS/ICBA). ## Key Question 6: Online sales rules under collegiate licenses (D2C, Amazon, marketplaces, domains/ads, MAP)

### Takeaway
A standard/retail license generally permits D2C and online sales, and online sales carry the same royalty/reporting obligations as wholesale (reported by SKU), but channel permissions are defined per license and per school (approved channel tiers such as campus, specialty, mid-tier, mass/club), co-brand and NIL products require separate approvals, and Amazon has become a sanctioned channel (Merch on Demand's NCAA program, Amazon winning CLC's 2025 retailer marketing award, schools linking to their own Amazon stores). No collegiate MAP policy, domain-name rule, or paid-search/Google Shopping rule was found in public sources.

### Cited Findings
- CLC Retail license: products "for sale at retail in approved retail channels and direct to consumer." — [CLC Get Licensed](https://clc.com/get-licensed)
- Oregon: "DTC sales are subject to the same royalty and compliance requirements as other sales channels"; "Retail Channel... include brick-and-mortar stores, e-commerce platforms, campus bookstores, and direct-to-consumer sales"; MTO products "must still be approved and are subject to standard licensing requirements"; the license agreement defines "product categories, retail channels, royalty rates, approval requirements, and compliance obligations." — [UO licensing definitions](https://communications.uoregon.edu/licensing-definitions)
- Internal licenses "do not permit sales to retailers or directly to consumers"; Retail/Standard: "Products can be sold on or off campus and by retailers and online stores." Local/Crafter licenses "typically limit where and how you can sell... Check with the Collegiate Licensing Company and the specific school to see if they permit selling through online platforms such as Etsy." — [Licensing International, Nov 2026](https://licensinginternational.org/news/what-to-do-after-getting-a-collegiate-license/); [CLW Local License](https://www.collegiatelicensingworld.com/what-is-a-clc-local-license)
- Channel tiers: Alabama's licensee list organizes approved licensees by channel including "Super Center/Wholesale Club" (Walmart, Target, Sam's, Costco), "Specialty Mass," and department store; schools run programs "in either middle-tier markets, at stores like Dick's... or in the mass market, at stores like Walmart" (2011). Nebraska maintains separate "Exclusive Mass Program" and "Exclusive Mass Headwear Program." — [Alabama licensing page](https://rolltide.com/sports/2016/6/10/licensing-purchasing-html); [Daily Tar Heel 2011](https://dailytarheel.com/article/2011/07/unc_merchandise_licenses_questioned); [Nebraska](https://licensing.unl.edu/how-become-licensed/guidelinescriteria-obtaining-license/)
- Amazon: Merch on Demand offers "over 40,000 NCAA-licensed products across more than 200 schools" via amazon.com/yourfanshop, produced "through NCAA licensees working with Amazon"; "Merch by Amazon" appears as a licensee on SFA's vendor list; George Mason links fans to its official Amazon store; CLC gave Amazon its 2025 Best Retailer Marketing Program award. — [SportBusiness](https://sportbusiness.com/organisation/amazon-merch-on-demand); [Licensing International](https://licensinginternational.org/news/amazon-merch-on-demand-has-launched-collection-of-officially-licensed-ncaa-merchandise-across-more-than-200-universities-and-colleges/); [SFA licensed vendors PDF](https://www.sfasu.edu/docs/procurement-business-services/collegiate-licensed-vendors.pdf); [GMU trademarks](https://brand.gmu.edu/trademarks); [CLC awards 2025](https://clc.com/clcs-2025-collegiate-licensing-awards-honor-organizations-for-elevating-college-brands/)
- Domain restriction (university-side): Oregon cannot sell on ".edu" sites and limits online sales of branded goods to shop.goducks.com. — [UO licensing policies](https://communications.uoregon.edu/node/676)
- Enforcement against unlicensed online sellers: Penn State v. Vintage Brand (filed 2021) — the online retailer's counterclaim that marks were "purely ornamental" survived dismissal; Ohio State sued an online marketplace. — [Klein Moynihan Turco](https://kleinmoynihan.com/trademark-law-infringement-universities-v-online-retailers); [Crowell client alert](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/a-house-built-on-sand-where-does-the-penn-state-case-leave-sports-merchandising); [National Law Review on Ohio State](https://natlawreview.com/article/ohio-state-university-seeing-scarlet-trademark-suit-against-online-marketplace)
- Sublicensing to decorators/printers is generally prohibited unless authorized in writing; a "Sell-Off Period" after expiration/termination requires notice and final royalty reporting. — [UO licensing definitions](https://communications.uoregon.edu/licensing-definitions)
- Comparison point (pro sports): the NFL requires retailer approval before selling on Amazon/Walmart.com third-party marketplaces; no equivalent collegiate rule found. — [Sports Litigation Alert](https://sportslitigationalert.com/federal-decision-examines-nfls-control-over-third-party-sellers-on-amazon-and-other-websites/)
- Kansas bases royalty on 50% of retail price when the licensee sells direct (i.e., D2C royalty base is imputed wholesale). — [KU policy](https://services.ku.edu/TDClient/818/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=21420)

### Inferences
- D2C/online is permitted for standard licensees but creates a reporting wrinkle: royalty base for D2C sales must be converted to an imputed wholesale (e.g., 50% of retail at Kansas) or reported on gross (Affinity, 12% of gross) depending on the school — another per-school rule that must live in the royalty engine.
- Online marketing content (product titles, ads, images using marks) likely falls under "advertising/promotional materials" that Nebraska requires be approved "prior to any production, distribution or sale," so listing copy and ad creative are approval-bearing artifacts, not just products.

### Gaps
- No public collegiate MAP policy, domain-name/SEO restriction, or Google Shopping/Meta ads rule for licensees was found.
- No "internet retailer" license category text was found (the CLC reference guide PDF was unreadable).
- No public rule on whether licensees may sell as third-party sellers on Fanatics.com or Walmart Marketplace was found.

## Key Question 7: Business model data — margins, inventory risk, MOQ, lead times, print-on-demand for long-tail schools

### Takeaway
Public data is thin. Royalties of 10–16% off wholesale sit on top of decoration cost; keystone (50% retail margin) is the generic retail benchmark; CLC categorizes the market as ~65% apparel/35% non-apparel; exclusives force inventory commitments (Top of the World raised Ohio State inventory from $3.5M to $5M for an exclusive); long-tail schools are increasingly served through made-to-order/POD models (Follett On-Demand 24–48 hr, One Chaptr's InkSoft stores, Amazon Merch on Demand) which licensors explicitly recognize ("Made-to-Order... must still be approved").

### Cited Findings
- Keystone pricing: retail = 2x wholesale (50% gross margin) — generic benchmark. — [RepSpark keystone explainer](https://www.repspark.com/keystone-pricing-explained-repspark)
- Market split: ~$4.6B retail, "roughly 65 percent attributed to apparel and 35 percent to non-apparel hardgoods" (CLC/IMG-era estimate, older). — [Merch Showroom summary of CLC data](https://merchshowroom.com/blogs/wholesale/collegiate-apparel-licensing-101-what-retailers-need-to-know-before-buying)
- Royalty burden on cost: budget "15–18% of your wholesale cost" for royalties (2026 guide). — [CLW 2026 guide](https://www.collegiatelicensingworld.com/how-you-can-get-an-officially-licensed-collegiate-product-to-market-in-2026-step-by-step-guide)
- Inventory risk from exclusives: Top of the World "increased the amount of Ohio State inventory it carries to $5 million from $3.5 million" after taking an exclusive (2017). — [Licensing International 2017](https://licensinginternational.org/news/colleges-licensees-seek-to-maximize-the-opportunity-via-exclusivity/)
- MOQ example: a licensed vendor asked a small UNC Charlotte buyer for a 50-unit minimum on decorated straw hats. — [Printavo/One Chaptr](https://www.printavo.com/blog/taking-the-licensed-apparel-market-by-storm-one-chaptr/)
- Store-side per-SKU minimums and inventory carrying cost are documented pain points for independents. — [PBS sidelines handout](https://assets.noviams.com/novi-file-uploads/pbs/pdfs-and-documents/Independent_Study/IS_Sidelines_Handout-6b9a2056.pdf)
- POD/MTO models: Follett On-Demand (in-house, 24–48 hr, no minimums); One Chaptr runs ~12 POD web stores and says POD beats "ordering 50 screen printed T-shirts and putting them in a closet"; Amazon Merch on Demand sells 40,000+ NCAA items across 200+ schools; NIL Store is piloting bookstore heat-press "blanks + decals" to carry athlete product without inventory. — [Follett On-Demand](https://follett.com/on-demand/); [Printavo](https://www.printavo.com/blog/taking-the-licensed-apparel-market-by-storm-one-chaptr/); [SportBusiness](https://sportbusiness.com/organisation/amazon-merch-on-demand); [ASI 2024](https://members.asicentral.com/news/strategy/august-2024/how-an-ncaa-settlement-could-change-the-college-athletics-merch-game-again/)
- Licensor recognition of MTO: "Made-to-Order (MTO): Products manufactured on demand after a customer places an order... MTO products must still be approved and are subject to standard licensing requirements." — [UO licensing definitions](https://communications.uoregon.edu/licensing-definitions)
- Local-license economics: upfront royalty, min ~$250, <2,000 units/yr — the licensor-side model for tiny-volume schools/sellers. — [CLW Local License](https://www.collegiatelicensingworld.com/what-is-a-clc-local-license)
- Fixed per-school costs regardless of volume: admin/renewal fees ($125–$200/school), advances ($200–$1,000/school at some schools), hologram/Logos-on-Demand subscription ($25–$50/school, capped $5,000), COI. — [CSUSB](https://www.csusb.edu/marketing-communications/csusb-identity-brand/trademarks-promotional-items/licensing-agreements); [Nebraska](https://licensing.unl.edu/how-become-licensed/guidelinescriteria-obtaining-license/); [UMass](https://www.massachusetts.edu/trademark-licensing/becoming-licensed/pricing-royalty-reporting)
- Lead time to first shipment for a new school: "6–9 months from first contact with the school to first shipment." — [CLW 2026 guide](https://www.collegiatelicensingworld.com/how-you-can-get-an-officially-licensed-collegiate-product-to-market-in-2026-step-by-step-guide)
- Mid-tier retail entrants compress the market: DICK'S private label (VRST/CALIA/DSG) collegiate launch (Nov 2026); Fanatics' CLC international deal gives Fanatics manufacturing + distribution + retail of college apparel outside the US. — [YSBR](https://youthsportsbusinessreport.com/dicks-brings-vrst-calia-and-dsg-into-collegiate-licensing-starting-with-ohio-state-and-iowa/); [CLW](https://www.collegiatelicensingworld.com/fanatics-inks-extended-collaboration-with-clc-for-global-distribution-of-us-college-merchandise)

### Inferences
- For a licensee with hundreds of schools, fixed per-school compliance costs (fees, advances, holograms, reporting labor) create a breakeven volume per school; below it, a school is a net cost unless served via MTO/POD with near-zero inventory. Deciding which schools to keep, drop, or convert to MTO is a data problem the licensee's own sales/royalty ledger can answer.
- Decorated hardgoods (drinkware, gifts) have fewer POD options than apparel (sublimation/laser/UV print are feasible in-house); the licensee's own factory is an advantage for MTO if artwork approval and order routing are automated.

### Gaps
- No sourced gross-margin figures for decorated licensed drinkware/gift licensees, typical bookstore markup on licensed hardgoods, or typical licensee MOQs/lead times to bookstores were found.
- No data on inventory turns or obsolescence rates for multi-school licensees.

## Key Question 8: Industry size and trends — market size, top schools, D2C growth, NIL co-licensing, conference realignment

### Takeaway
Collegiate licensed merchandise is a ~$3.5–4.6B US retail category (estimates vary by source and are dated 2014–2018); CLC reports partner-school licensed sales +11% and royalties +16% in Jan–Jun 2026. Texas, Alabama, Georgia, Michigan, Ohio State lead (estimates); Texas/Oklahoma SEC moves and USC/Oregon/Washington Big Ten moves are credited in 2025–26 rankings. NIL group licensing (Brandr Group, OneTeam/Fanatics) adds an athlete-rights layer with widely varying athlete royalties (~4% to 20–30%), and agencies now bundle NIL into long-term school deals (Kentucky/Fanatics through 2036–38).

### Cited Findings
- Market size: CLC/IMG estimated $4.6B retail (2014; repeated through 2018 and in 2026 secondary sources); The Licensing Letter survey: $3.4B (2014), $3.5B (2016), $3.6B (2017); apparel ~65%. — [Bleacher Report 2012 ($4.6B)](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/1290567-texas-and-kentucky-top-college-rankings-in-the-46-billion-licensing-industry); [Glossy 2018 ($4.6B via IMG)](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/college-fans-are-very-loyal-why-fashion-brands-are-launching-collegiate-apparel/); [The Licensing Letter](https://www.thelicensingletter.com/?p=1772)
- Global licensed merchandise +5.45% in 2025 to $389.8B; sports is the third-largest property category at 11.4% (Licensing International 2026 study). — [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/licensed-merchandise-reports-continued-growth-in-2025/2026052072459)
- CLC partner institutions: "11% growth in sales of licensed merchandise, while royalties were up 16%" (Jan 1–Jun 30 2026); 9% sales growth in H1 FY2026; Omaha's wholesale sales +41% and royalties +40% since 2022. — [CLC 29/31-institution extension release, Jul 2026](https://clc.com/clc-executes-multiyear-extensions-with-31-collegiate-institutions-for-brand-licensing-representation/); [CLC Omaha release](https://clc.com/omaha-athletics-extends-partnership-with-clc/)
- Top schools (official CLC rankings have not been published since 2012–13; CLW's 2025 list is an estimate from 2012–13 data + football/basketball performance): 1 Texas, 2 Alabama, 3 Georgia, 4 Michigan, 5 Ohio State, 6 Notre Dame, 7 LSU, 8 Texas A&M, 9 Oklahoma, 10 Florida; drivers cited include "SEC move" (Texas, Oklahoma), "Big Ten move" (USC, Washington), "Nike-backed; uniform/merch appeal" (Oregon), NIL stars (Caitlin Clark at Iowa, Zach Edey at Purdue). — [CLW 2025 ranking](https://www.collegiatelicensingworld.com/2025-ranking-top-75-colleges-by-merchandise-licensing-revenue); [CLW 2012–13 official ranking](https://www.collegiatelicensingworld.com/collegiate-licensing-top-selling-universities-and-vendors)
- Ohio State's program was ~$15M/yr in 2017; Texas is estimated at "~$10M+" royalties in 2025 by an unsourced secondary site. — [Licensing International 2017](https://licensinginternational.org/news/colleges-licensees-seek-to-maximize-the-opportunity-via-exclusivity/); [Academic Jobs (unverified)](https://www.academicjobs.com/higher-education-news/top-20-us-college-merch-brands-by-sales-2026-or-academicjobs-11757)
- Conference realignment: Texas and Oklahoma fans got their first SEC-branded school merchandise at the switch; licensing projections credit SEC entry and Big Ten moves for ranking gains; realignment also moves a school's conference/post-season hot-market program (e.g., Oregon now works with CLC for Big Ten programs while FLM is its primary agency). — [CBS Austin](https://cbsaustin.com/sports/college/bye-to-the-big-12-and-hello-sec-texas-and-oklahoma-party-as-conference-move-now-official); [CLW 2026 projections](https://www.collegiatelicensingworld.com/2026-college-football-merchandise-licensing-projections-and-rankings); [UO licensing definitions](https://communications.uoregon.edu/licensing-definitions)
- NIL group licensing: The Brandr Group (70+ licensees, athletes at 80+ colleges; Michigan, Ohio, Syracuse, BYU, UVA, Buffalo, Oklahoma State, Pitt programs) pays athletes ~$10–12/jersey (~10% of invoice); OneTeam/Fanatics' 4,300-athlete jersey program paid ~$3.92 on a $140 jersey (<5%, 2022); The M Den/Valiant ~$20/jersey; NIL Store 20–30%. Co-branded NIL product "require[s] school-mark rights from the university or its licensing agency, while athlete NIL rights are handled separately. Athlete participation can also change with transfers or graduation." — [Front Office Sports, Sept 2022](https://frontofficesports.com/article/below-market-rate-fanatics-cfb/); [On3 Michigan/Brandr](https://www.on3.com/teams/michigan-wolverines/news/michigan-partners-with-the-brandr-group-to-launch-group-licensing-for-student-athletes); [Licensing International Aug 2026](https://licensinginternational.org/news/what-brands-need-to-get-right-in-collegiate-merchandise/); [ASI 2024](https://members.asicentral.com/news/strategy/august-2024/how-an-ncaa-settlement-could-change-the-college-athletics-merch-game-again/)
- NIL Store license rule: product may be co-branded with the university logo "but it must also include a specific athlete's name, image or other identifying criteria"; athletes must opt in to Brandr or Campus Ink programs (Utah). — [ASI 2024](https://members.asicentral.com/news/strategy/august-2024/how-an-ncaa-settlement-could-change-the-college-athletics-merch-game-again/); [Utah NIL store](https://www.store.utah.edu/custom-nil-store)
- OneTeam acquired a minority stake in The Brandr Group (April, year per On3) to collaborate on college group licensing; Octane5's BrandComply is Brandr's platform for contract management, approvals and royalty reporting. — [On3 group licensing Q&A](https://www.on3.com/nil/news/group-licensing-nil-potential-impact-question-and-answers/); [Licensing International Octane5/Brandr](https://licensinginternational.org/news/octane5-enters-the-nil-licensing-game-with-the-brandr-group/)
- Agencies bundling NIL into long-term school deals: Kentucky extended with Fanatics (reported through 2036 or 2038 — sources conflict) with an NIL component launching spring 2026; Follett On-Demand "leverage[s] relationships with all NIL licensing agencies." — [Youth Sports Business Report on UK/Fanatics](https://youthsportsbusinessreport.com/university-of-kentucky-and-fanatics-build-nil-into-long-term-merchandise-deal); [Follett On-Demand](https://follett.com/on-demand/)
- Fanatics/Topps college trading cards program with 100+ university partnerships; EA SPORTS named CLC's 2025 non-apparel IMPACT winner (College Football video game revival drives licensing). — [Licensing International on Fanatics/Topps](https://licensinginternational.org/news/fanatics-collectibles-and-topps-announce-comprehensive-college-trading-cards-program-with-more-than-100-new-university-partnerships/); [CLC awards 2025](https://clc.com/clcs-2025-collegiate-licensing-awards-honor-organizations-for-elevating-college-brands/)
- D2C/online: Follett runs 1,800 campus e-commerce sites; Fanatics powers BNED's ~800 store e-commerce; Amazon Merch on Demand covers 200+ schools; a market tracker reported aggregate online university-merch sales across tracked retailers down 20–50% in 2025 but expects 0–5% growth in 2026 (tracker covers only its monitored retailers). — [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2020/08/13/college-bookstore-operator-follett-is-learning-on-the-fly-this-unusual-back-to-school-shopping-season); [SportBusiness](https://sportbusiness.com/news/fanatics-lids-expand-college-sports-presence-with-barnes-noble-education-partnership); [Grips Intelligence tracker](https://gripsintelligence.com/insights/industries/apparel-accessories/university-and-college-merchandise)
- Fashion/collab trend: College Vault vintage program (launched 2005) resurged; "College has really become a lifestyle brand... college is year-round" (IMG, 2018); Sanrio/Blue 84 Hello Kitty collegiate; JanSport/L2; Buc-ee's/Texas A&M; Dolly Parton/Tennessee; Howard, Tennessee, Virginia Tech won CLC's 2025 "Best Product Partnership or Collaboration." — [Glossy 2018](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/college-fans-are-very-loyal-why-fashion-brands-are-launching-collegiate-apparel/); [CLC awards 2025](https://clc.com/clcs-2025-collegiate-licensing-awards-honor-organizations-for-elevating-college-brands/)
- Licensors are pushing data: UH chose CLC for "proprietary and industry-leading data systems, providing the sales trends and consumer insights"; CLC awards a "Best Use of Insights and Data" category (2025 winners Texas A&M, UConn, Ball State). — [UH news 2026](https://www.uh.edu/news-events/stories/2026/january/01142026-uh-new-licensing-partner.php); [CLC awards 2025](https://clc.com/clcs-2025-collegiate-licensing-awards-honor-organizations-for-elevating-college-brands/)

### Inferences
- The addressable upside for a multi-school licensee is increasingly in (a) NIL co-branded product, which doubles the approval/rights complexity (school + athlete + opt-in status + roster changes), and (b) collaborations/lifestyle product, which the agencies reward with awards and exclusives; both require faster, more reliable approval and reporting plumbing than the legacy quarterly-spreadsheet model.
- Realignment events are time-boxed hot markets (new conference logos, "first SEC season" merch) that require rapid per-school artwork approval and sometimes a second agency relationship (conference/post-season programs via CLC).

### Gaps
- No current (2024–2026) primary-source US collegiate market-size figure was found; all are 2014–2018 estimates or global/aggregate.
- No official CLC top-school ranking after 2012–13; 2025 lists are estimates.
- Kentucky/Fanatics deal term length conflicts across sources (2036 vs 2038) and the "$40M/yr" claim is from an unreliable aggregator.
- No quantitative study of conference realignment's effect on licensee sales was found.
