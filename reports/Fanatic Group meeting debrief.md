# Four Manual Gates Throttle Fanatic's Floor

*Meeting debrief and build plan following the 18 Nov 2026 in-person meeting (see the Addendum near the end for what the shop-floor photos changed) with Gregg Solomon, President of The Fanatic Group (Edison, NJ). The raw meeting notes are the authoritative description of the current workflow and supersede pre-meeting inferences; research facts carry inline citations; consultant judgments are labelled as such.*

The meeting moved the center of gravity of this engagement from the Shopify storefront to the inside of NetSuite. Gregg described four manual gates that every order passes through: web, TikTok and small-account orders are re-keyed into NetSuite by hand; every sales order is manually approved; every sales line becomes a work order that Gregg personally releases to production; and shipping labels are keyed by hand at up to **500 shipments a day at 1 to 5 minutes each**, which is **8 to 42 labor-hours a day at peak** (meeting notes, 18 Nov 2026). Between the release gate and the label gate sits the art path, where one artist (Steve) locates the right file among roughly a million Dropbox files and re-prepares it in whichever format the target machine needs, before licensing and customer approvals. The research shows that three of the four gates are configuration and connector problems rather than AI problems: the approval gate is NetSuite's "Default Sales Order Status" accounting preference plus the fact that a pre-approved order can be created via REST with `orderStatus = "B"` ([Oracle Help](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_159795516182.html)); the release gate is the Planned-to-Released work-order status transition that can be defaulted or scripted ([Oracle Help](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N1388149.html)); the label gate is OzLINK's design as a bridge for people who "already use and love carrier shipping software," which makes a human finish each shipment in UPS WorldShip or FedEx Ship Manager ([Descartes](https://www.descartes.com/solutions/ecommerce-shipping-fulfillment/warehouse-management/descartes-ozlink-shipping-for-netsuite)); and the re-keying gate exists because no Shopify-to-NetSuite connector is installed, when Oracle's own connector starts at **$199.92/month** on the Shopify App Store ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/oracle-netsuite)). The genuinely novel build is Gregg's "life-changing" ask, and the research supports a design that avoids OCR for the identification step entirely: NetSuite's Advanced PDF templates can print a QR code carrying the work-order internal ID ([Oracle Help](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2868543.html)), so a scan pulls every structured field from NetSuite, and a self-built Dropbox index (metadata crawl, Ghostscript thumbnails, parsed filenames, CLIP embeddings) ranks candidate art files, while vision extraction is kept as a fallback for handwritten annotations and legacy travelers. The recommended 90 days therefore run two tracks in parallel: a configuration-and-connector track that removes the approval, release, order-entry and label gates with mostly bought or native components, and a build track that delivers the QR traveler, art finder, format conversion and two-stage approval tracker. The Owala storefront and catalog work from the pre-meeting report stays on the table but moves behind the floor work, because the floor is what caps everything the storefront could sell.

## What changed: the bottleneck is release and re-keying, not demand

The pre-meeting report was written without knowing the system of record. Its discovery table literally asked "what runs orders, inventory and accounting today (QuickBooks? an OMS? Printavo/ShopWorks?)" and its solution designs targeted Shopify Draft Orders, Shopify metafields and Shopify inventory as the integration surface. The meeting answered the question with NetSuite as the ERP and order system of record, Shopify as the D2C storefront with TikTok Shop orders flowing into it, OzLINK (Descartes, formerly Oz Development) for shipping communication, and Dropbox for the art library (meeting notes, 18 Nov 2026). That single fact re-points almost every design: orders must land in NetSuite, not Shopify; approvals and work orders are NetSuite records; inventory truth lives in NetSuite's assemblies and components rather than in a Shopify app; and any future royalty engine reads the NetSuite ledger, not Shopify's orders API. It also means the "blank-aware inventory" idea, which the earlier report treated as a Shopify modelling gap, is largely solved inside NetSuite already (special-order work orders consume component blanks) and reduces to syncing NetSuite availability back to Shopify.

The second change is in priority. The pre-meeting report ranked twelve ideas by leverage and licensing safety, leading with Owala page triage and a catalog data layer because the storefront defects were the only things observable from outside. Gregg's own account puts the pain in a different place: throughput is capped by his personal availability to release every sales line's work order, by the art desk, and by a label step that consumes between one and five full-time equivalents on a peak day (meeting notes, 18 Nov 2026). ## The current workflow has a root cause at every manual step

The ten steps below are the order-to-ship path as Gregg described it, each paired with the mechanism the research identifies as the reason it is manual today. The root causes are inferences to be verified in Gregg's account, but each rests on documented NetSuite, OzLINK or connector behavior.

| # | Step as described by Gregg | What is happening today | Root cause the research points to |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Web, TikTok Shop and small-account orders are keyed into NetSuite by hand | Someone reads a Shopify order (including TikTok orders that flow into Shopify) and re-enters customer, items and ship-to into a NetSuite sales order | **No Shopify-to-NetSuite connector is installed.** Shopify has no first-party NetSuite integration; the packaged options are Oracle's own "NetSuite ERP Connector" (ex-FarApp) from $199.92/month, Celigo (≈US$7,800/yr starting package), Pipe17 ($24,000/yr), or a custom webhook-to-REST build ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/oracle-netsuite); [Celigo listing](https://apps.shopify.com/celigo-netsuite-smartconnector); [Pipe17](https://apps.shopify.com/pipe17-app); [Houseblend](https://www.houseblend.io/articles/en/guideintegrationnetsuiteshopify)). |
| 2 | Every step of every order is manually approved in NetSuite | Each sales order lands in Pending Approval and a person approves it, possibly again after any edit | **Accounting preference "Default Sales Order Status" is set to Pending Approval**, and possibly "Require Re-approval on Edit of Sales Order" is on; Oracle's help says to choose Pending Fulfillment "if your company doesn't use the approval process" ([Oracle Help: Order Management preferences](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N1388149.html)). | 3 | Every sales line becomes a work order | A linked "special order" work order is created per assembly line | This is the correct native behavior: when the line's Create WO box is checked (defaulted by the item's Special Work Order Item flag), "when you save or approve the order, NetSuite creates a work order that is linked to the sale" and adds it to the Mass Create Work Orders queue ([Oracle Help](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/article_161538656149.html); [Oracle Help](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/article_161538684793.html)). Release is a legitimate gate ("releasing too early floods WIP") but nothing requires the owner to be the one clicking ([Broken Rubik](https://www.brokenrubik.com/blog/netsuite-work-orders-guide)). |
| 5 | Order goes to the art department in NetSuite and lands in Steve's queue | A paper traveler (work order printout) reaches the pre-press desk | How the traveler is printed is unknown (stock Work Order PDF, custom Advanced PDF template, or Advanced Manufacturing traveler) and whether it carries a barcode is unknown (meeting notes). Without Advanced Manufacturing, shops customize the Work Order advanced PDF template to make a traveler ([NetSuite Professionals archive](https://archive.netsuiteprofessionals.com/t/8552577/anyone-know-if-there-is-a-native-way-to-make-a-work-order-tr)). |
| 6 | Steve locates the right art file among ~1M Dropbox files and produces the machine-specific format | Manual search by folder/filename memory, open in Illustrator, re-save in the format the machine wants | Dropbox's own search is not fit for this: `files/search_v2` is tokenized rather than substring, non-exhaustive and lagged, and the thumbnails API renders only jpg/png/tiff/gif/bmp under 20 MB, not .ai/.eps/.pdf ([Dropbox Community](https://community.dropbox.com/en/discussion/765443/python-sdk-api-inconsistent-results-with-files-search-v2); [Dropbox migration guide](https://www.dropbox.com/developers/reference/migration-guide)). Each machine wants a different input (Inkcups: vectored PDF/EPS/TIFF or a layered PNG via ArtPrep; Trotec: .ai/.cdr/.svg vector; Epilog: .eps/.ai to cut; Sawgrass: JPG/PNG/TIFF/PDF RGB) ([Inkcups](https://www.inkcups.com/uv-printing-technology-explained/); [Trotec](https://www.troteclaser.com/en-us/resources/faqs/compatible-graphics-programs); [Epilog](https://www.epiloglaser.com/how-it-works/faq/what-types-of-graphics-can-i-engrave-and-cut); [Sawgrass](https://supportdesk.sawgrassink.com/en/support/discussions/topics/65000197933)). |

| 8 | Art goes to the customer for approval | Proof emailed; approval tracked by hand | No decorator tool models licensor-then-customer approval with expiry and NetSuite write-back; Printavo and GraphicsFlow do customer e-approvals only ([Printavo](https://www.printavo.com/pricing/); [GraphicsFlow](https://www.graphicsflow.com/)). |
| 9 | Once both approved, the order is "ready for production" and follows the path | Someone re-checks approvals and routes the job | The approval states are not fields on the NetSuite work order, so the release rule in step 4 cannot read them (inference). |

Make any necessary changes in your shipping software, then click Process Shipment to print a shipping label" ([Oz Development help](https://ozdevelopment.com/help/ozlinkforups/3.6/shipping_from_netsuite.htm)). Descartes positions it for shops that "already use and love carrier shipping software" ([Descartes](https://www.descartes.com/solutions/ecommerce-shipping-fulfillment/warehouse-management/descartes-ozlink-shipping-for-netsuite)). Missing item weights/dims and ship-method mappings have to be typed in the carrier UI, which is where the minutes go (inference). |

The labor math on step 10 alone justifies the engagement. At 500 shipments and 1 to 5 minutes each, the label step costs **500 to 2,500 minutes, or 8.3 to 41.7 hours, per peak day**; at a 2-minute average that is **16.7 hours, roughly two full-time people, doing nothing but typing addresses into carrier software** (arithmetic from the meeting notes). The vendor case studies show the same toolset running at that volume without the manual step when item data and scanning are clean: Descartes cites Mountain Khakis at "400-500 shipments per day during peak times" and HealthGenesis handling 400 orders a day with a 42% operational performance increase and more than $37,500 in annual savings ([Descartes](https://www.descartes.com/resources/knowledge-center/optimizing-omnichannel-order-fulfillment-descartes-ozlinktm); [CaseStudies.com](https://www.casestudies.com/company/descartes/case-study/healthgenesiscom-energizing-fulfillment-with-descartes-ozlinktm-for-netsuite)). Both figures are vendor-published, but they establish that 1 to 5 minutes per label is a configuration and data problem, not a product ceiling.

The art-desk math is softer because Gregg did not quantify it, and the research found no decorator-specific benchmark. The nearest proxies are a DAM case study reporting "10 to 15 minutes per search" saved and print-shop designers describing per-job budgets of "5–15 minutes" ([casestudies.com](https://www.casestudies.com/company/daminion/case-study/floyd-county-productions-saves-10-to-15-minutes-per-search-with-daminion); [r/graphic_design](https://www.reddit.com/r/graphic_design/comments/1rlo0gp/huge_increase_in_ai_generated_slop/)). The right move is to measure the baseline directly in the first two weeks: time-stamp when a traveler reaches the art desk and when the correct master file is opened, for every job, and let that number set the business case.

## Target workflow: orders flow in approved, release is a rule, labels print themselves

The target state keeps NetSuite as the system of record and adds one custom web application (the "art finder") plus a small number of scripts, a connector, and a shipping integration. Nothing in it requires Gregg to click on an individual order unless a rule flags an exception.

An order placed on Shopify, including a TikTok Shop order that Shopify receives, reaches NetSuite within minutes through a connector or a webhook-driven RESTlet. Because the order is prepaid, it is created with `orderStatus = "B"` (Pending Fulfillment), the documented way "to create a preapproved sales order" ([Oracle Help](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_159795516182.html)); wholesale and net-terms orders are created as "A" (Pending Approval) and a workflow action script approves those that pass credit-limit and customer-trust rules, leaving only true exceptions for a human. Each decorated line is an assembly item flagged Special Work Order Item, so saving or approving the order drops a linked work order into the Mass Create Work Orders queue ([Oracle Help](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/article_161538684793.html)). Work orders are born in Planned status on purpose, as the hold that the art path releases.

The traveler printed from the Work Order advanced PDF template carries a QR code encoding a URL with the work order's internal ID, using NetSuite's native `<barcode codetype="qrcode" ...>` tag ([Oracle Help](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2868543.html)). At the art desk, Steve scans it with a phone camera or a desk scanner and the art finder opens that work order: it pulls customer, item SKU, school/licensor, mark type, imprint color and location from NetSuite via REST, runs a structured query against the Dropbox index, and shows ranked thumbnails of candidate master files alongside the traveler image and the existing production-ready variants for the target machine. Steve picks a file (or confirms the top candidate), and the conversion service produces the machine-specific output (PDF/EPS for the UV cylinder printer, SVG/.ai for the laser, RGB PNG for sublimation) into a per-machine hot folder, attaches the chosen file reference to the work order, and renders a proof. If the design, licensor and product combination already has an unexpired licensing approval in the tracker, the licensing leg is skipped; otherwise the tracker pre-fills a Brand Manager 360 submission (`POST Artwork/SubmitArtwork` with license code, design number, product colors, retailer, product combinations and the rendered art file) and polls `GetSubmittedArtQueueById` for the decision ([BM360 API](https://clientapiqa.brandmanager360.com/Help/Api/POST-Artwork-SubmitArtwork)); non-CLC schools get a "mark as submitted" step. The customer leg is an e-approval link with the proof, approve/request-changes buttons and reminder emails.

When both approvals exist, the tracker writes two custom checkbox fields on the work order (`custbody_tfg_lic_approved`, `custbody_tfg_cust_approved`) and the art-file reference. A scheduled Map/Reduce script, Oracle's recommended type "for any scenario where you want to process multiple records" ([Oracle Help](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4387799161.html)), releases every Planned work order whose flags are true and whose components are available; Gregg supervises exceptions on the 2026.2 Production Control Board, whose "Work Orders to Release" and "At Risk" subtabs and 200-per-submission bulk release replace his per-record clicks ([Houseblend on 2026.2](https://www.houseblend.io/articles/netsuite-production-control-board-work-order-triage)). Production completes the build, the item fulfillment is created, and shipping runs through a label engine that reads weights and dimensions from the item record and prints without re-keying: either ShipStation's direct NetSuite connection with automation rules and tracking write-back ([Houseblend on ShipStation](https://www.houseblend.io/articles/shipstation-netsuite-direct-connection-vs-connector)), or NetSuite Ship Central's pack-and-ship on the item fulfillment, or OzLINK kept but fed complete item data and a scale so the carrier-software step is one click. Tracking flows back to NetSuite and from there to Shopify through the connector's fulfillment sync, closing the loop with the customer and with TikTok Shop.

The order of operations matters as much as the components. The approval and release rules are safe only once the art path writes its approval flags, and the label automation is safe only once item weights and dimensions are populated; so the first weeks are data and template work, not scripts.

## Revised solution set: five gates, one true AI build, one copilot

Each card below gives the problem, the technical design, buy-versus-build, what is needed from Gregg, the expected impact with its evidence, effort, risks and a quick-win or bigger-build tag. Effort estimates are consultant judgment, not sourced benchmarks. The cards are in build order, which is also roughly impact order once dependencies are respected.

### Solution 1. Shopify and TikTok Shop orders into NetSuite without re-keying (quick win via connector; medium if custom)

**Problem.** All web and TikTok Shop orders and all small-account orders are keyed into NetSuite by hand (meeting notes). Volume is unknown and must be measured, but every one of these orders is also the start of the approval and work-order chain, so the keying delay propagates.

**Technical design.** Two viable paths. *Path A (buy):* install Oracle's "NetSuite ERP Connector" (ex-FarApp) from the Shopify App Store, B2C Standard tier at **$199.92/month** with order sync (real-time option), fulfillment sync and price/quantity sync, or B2C Premium at $249.92/month for real-time order and inventory sync ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/oracle-netsuite)). Map Shopify order tags or `source_name` (TikTok orders arrive in Shopify carrying their channel) to a NetSuite custom field or Class so TikTok revenue stays attributable; no vendor document found describes this mapping explicitly, so it must be tested. Configure the connector to create orders as Pending Fulfillment for paid orders (see Solution 2). *Path B (build):* a Shopify `orders/create` webhook into a small middle tier (Cloud Run or a VPS) that matches customer by email, matches items by SKU against NetSuite item records, and POSTs a sales order to SuiteTalk REST with `"orderStatus": "B"`; fulfillments and tracking are written back to Shopify via Admin GraphQL. NetSuite 2026.1's REST "homogeneous batch operations" allow several same-type creates in one asynchronous call, which helps at peak ([NetSuite 2026.1](https://www.netsuite.com/portal/resource/articles/cloud-saas/suitecloud-platform-delivers-ai-native-development-expanded-rest-apis-and-next-generation-extensibility-in-netsuite-2026-1.shtml)). Either path must respect NetSuite's concurrency pool: a Standard service tier with no SuiteCloud Plus license allows **5 concurrent integration requests** shared by every integration, including OzLINK/ShipStation and any MCP client ([Oracle Help: SuiteCloud Plus](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N259503.html)).

**Buy vs build.** Start with the Oracle connector: cheapest, sold through NetSuite, and the throughput figure of "25–200 transactions per hour per data type" is enough for 500 orders spread over a day (about 21 per hour) though not for a flash burst ([Houseblend](https://www.houseblend.io/articles/en/guideintegrationnetsuiteshopify)). Its reviews warn it is "very difficult to manage" and that successful merchants used a consultancy to configure it ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/oracle-netsuite)); that configuration is the consultant's job here. Escalate to Celigo (CAD $11,153.84/yr starting package, about US$7,800; also has a direct TikTok Shop connector) only if settlement reconciliation from TikTok Shop must happen in NetSuite ([Celigo listing](https://apps.shopify.com/celigo-netsuite-smartconnector); [SixLakes](https://www.sixlakesconsulting.com/blogs/tiktok-shop-netsuite-integration-celigo.html)). Pipe17 at $24,000/yr is over-scoped ([Pipe17](https://apps.shopify.com/pipe17-app)).

**Data and access needed.** Shopify admin (collaborator access) and confirmation of which TikTok Shop app feeds Shopify; NetSuite administrator or an integration role with REST Web Services and OAuth 2.0; the SKU-parity check between Shopify variant SKUs and NetSuite item names (one rollout cited by Houseblend saw "30% of initial order syncs failed due to SKU mismatches"); the customer-matching rule (email) to avoid the duplicate-customer problem documented by Celigo and the Oracle community ([Celigo Help](https://docs.celigo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002722448-Why-is-my-Shopify-NetSuite-Integration-App-Creating-Duplicate-Customers-in-NetSuite); [Oracle Community](https://community.oracle.com/netsuite/english/discussion/4487123/shopify-connector-that-can-do-customer-mapping)).

**Impact and evidence.** Removes 100% of web/TikTok keying. Vendor cases: eyebobs "eliminated nearly all manual data entry" and saved roughly $200,000 in labor after moving to Celigo; Tone It Up went "from double entry to full automation" ([Houseblend](https://www.houseblend.io/articles/en/guideintegrationnetsuiteshopify)). Labor math requires Gregg's order counts; if web plus TikTok is even 100 orders/day at 3 minutes each, that is 5 hours/day (assumption, to be replaced with measured data).

**Effort.** 2–4 weeks for the Oracle connector with SKU cleanup; 4–6 weeks for a custom RESTlet build with retries and monitoring.

**Risks.** Duplicate customers and SKU mismatches are the universal failure mode; run in a sandbox with a replay of last month's Shopify orders first. Do not cut over during Black Friday week.

### Solution 2. Approval by exception instead of approval of everything (quick win)

**Problem.** Every sales order is manually approved, and every edit may re-trigger approval (meeting notes).

**Technical design.** First confirm the two accounting preferences at Setup > Accounting > Accounting Preferences > Order Management: "Default Sales Order Status" (Pending Approval vs Pending Fulfillment) and "Require Re-approval on Edit of Sales Order" ([Oracle Help](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N1388149.html)). If Gregg wants no approval on prepaid D2C orders, the connector creates them as "B" and the preference governs only hand-entered orders. For wholesale: keep Pending Approval as the default and deploy a SuiteFlow workflow on Sales Order (Create and Edit, with Web Store and Web Application contexts enabled, since those are "omitted from workflows and actions by default in the context field") whose conditions check customer credit limit, open balance, order total threshold and a "trusted account" checkbox, then call a Workflow Action Script that does `so.setValue({fieldId:'orderstatus', value:'B'})`, the practitioner workaround for SuiteFlow's inability to set `orderstatus` directly ([NetSuite Professionals archive](https://archive.netsuiteprofessionals.com/t/34058598/suitescript-to-set-sales-order-status-to-approved-and-pendin); [archive](https://archive.netsuiteprofessionals.com/t/33091175/we-allow-consumers-to-purchase-from-our-website-but-our-sale)). Orders that fail the rule stay Pending Approval and appear on the native Approve Sales Orders page, which supports bulk approval and a "Security Match" column for AVS/CSC on card orders ([Oracle Help: Approving Sales Orders](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N1218788.html)). Hold card-payment failures in "A" explicitly, because API-pushed orders with failed authorization otherwise land in Pending Fulfillment ([Oracle Community](https://community.oracle.com/netsuite/english/discussion/4227563/automatically-set-status-of-sales-orders-on-payment-hold-to-pending-approval)).

**Buy vs build.** Build; it is one preference change and one short script. Oracle's SuiteApprovals SuiteApp auto-approves when "the submitter's sales order entry limit exceeds the transaction amount" and is an alternative if Gregg prefers a packaged approval matrix ([Folio3](https://netsuite.folio3.com/blog/netsuite-suiteapprovals-boosting-productivity-through-automated-approvals/)).

**Data and access needed.** Administrator access to preferences; a list of the rules Gregg actually applies when he approves (credit, trust, minimums, licensing status of the school); sandbox.

**Impact and evidence.** Removes the approval click from every prepaid order and from every rule-passing wholesale order; the Six Lakes threshold pattern releases rule-passing orders "immediately" ([Six Lakes](https://www.sixlakesconsulting.com/ns-solutions/netsuite-sales-order-approval-thresholds.html)). Labor saved is minutes per order times order count; the larger effect is that approval stops being a serial dependency on one person.

**Effort.** 2–5 days including sandbox testing.

**Risks.** One poster reports "Invalid orderstatus reference key B" errors in some contexts ([archive](https://archive.netsuiteprofessionals.com/t/30323903/how-to-approve-sales-order-via-suite-script)); test on each entry path (UI, connector, REST). Confirm the exact permission that governs Approve.

### Solution 3. Work orders that create themselves and release by rule (quick win, then medium)

**Problem.** Every sales line's work order waits for Gregg to release it (meeting notes).

**Technical design.** Confirm each decorated assembly item has Special Work Order Item checked so lines default Create WO; note that community reports say the Create WO box "can only be set when the line is first created" and kit lines do not create WOs, so audit item types ([NetSuite Professionals archive](https://archive.netsuiteprofessionals.com/t/23090948/automatically-create-wo-from-sales-order-lines)). Then choose one of two release models. *Model A (simplest):* set "Default Work Order Status" to Released so special-order WOs are born Released and components commit immediately ([Oracle Help](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N1388149.html)); this abolishes the gate and is appropriate only if art approval is tracked elsewhere. *Model B (recommended):* keep Planned as the hold, add custom fields to the Work Order (`custbody_tfg_lic_approved`, `custbody_tfg_cust_approved`, `custbody_tfg_art_file`, `custbody_tfg_machine`), and run a scheduled Map/Reduce every 15 minutes that searches Planned WOs with both approvals true and components available and sets them Released, mirroring what the Mark Work Orders Released page does ([Oracle Help](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/article_161538605076.html)). The exact scriptable field for Planned-to-Released must be confirmed in sandbox; no Oracle page read in this research documents scripted release. Gregg's oversight moves to the Production Control Board (2026.2, September 2026), which groups WOs into Active, At Risk, Ready, In Process, Work Orders to Release and Work Orders to Close, flags component shortages and late starts, and bulk-releases up to 200 per submission ([Houseblend](https://www.houseblend.io/articles/netsuite-production-control-board-work-order-triage)).

**Data and access needed.** Which modules are licensed (Manufacturing WIP & Routings, Advanced Manufacturing); the preference screen; the rules Gregg applies when he decides to release (art approved, components in stock, due date, customer priority); confirmation of the 2026.2 upgrade in his account.

**Impact and evidence.** Gregg stops being the single point of release for every sales line, which the meeting notes identify as the throughput cap. ### Solution 4. Labels at 500 a day without typing (quick win, largest labor line)

**Problem.** Label creation is manual data entry at up to 500 shipments a day, 1 to 5 minutes each, running through NetSuite plus OzLINK (meeting notes). That is 8.3 to 41.7 labor-hours per peak day.

**Technical design.** Three options, in order of preference on current evidence. *Option A: ShipStation direct connection.* ShipStation's new direct NetSuite connection uses OAuth 2.0 and the NetSuite account ID, "supports Sales Orders, PickPackShip (Picked), PickPackShip (Packed), and Transfer Orders," allows one order filter and three extra mapped fields, is offered "at no additional cost on every ShipStation plan through the end of 2026," and ShipStation US plans run **$14.99 (Starter), $29.99 (Standard) and $349.99 (Premium) per month** ([Houseblend](https://www.houseblend.io/articles/shipstation-netsuite-direct-connection-vs-connector)). Pull built/ready orders into ShipStation, apply automation rules (preset weight by SKU, service by destination, auto-print to a thermal printer), batch-print, and let tracking write back to the NetSuite fulfillment. This is different from Oracle's older ShipStation Connector path, which defaults item-fulfillment weight to 1 lb, does not sync weight and only considers Packed fulfillments ([Oracle Help](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_163162030389.html)). *Option B: NetSuite Ship Central.* A paid SuiteApp built on ShipEngine; requires Pick, Pack and Ship and Shipping Label Integration features, a ShipEngine account created in NetSuite and Integrated Shipping Mode on the order; labels print from the item fulfillment for FedEx/UPS/USPS, with PrintNode for direct thermal printing; bulk shipping in the kiosk app handles 10 shipments per batch ([Oracle Help: Direct Packing or Shipping](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_0324093230.html); [Oracle Help: Bulk Shipping](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_0717014902.html)). Price is not public ([Broken Rubik](https://www.brokenrubik.com/blog/netsuite-ship-central-guide)). Note that NetSuite's legacy FedEx/UPS/USPS integration "is not available to new customers" ([Oracle Help](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N1263996.html)). *Option C: keep OzLINK, fix the data.* Populate item weights and dimensions, map Translate Ship Vias, add a USB scale (Descartes markets scale automation and barcode verification), so the WorldShip/FSM step becomes a single Process Shipment click; Descartes also sells Descartes ShipRush for NetSuite on the same page ([Descartes](https://www.descartes.com/solutions/ecommerce-shipping-fulfillment/warehouse-management/descartes-ozlink-shipping-for-netsuite)). Whatever the option, the prerequisite is the same: **every sellable item needs weight and package dimensions on its NetSuite item record**, and a default carton logic per product family (one tumbler, two tumblers, case) must be defined.

**Buy vs build.** Buy. The build portion is item data hygiene and a short script that sets a "ready to ship" status when the work order is Built and all lines are complete.

**Data and access needed.** OzLINK version and edition (the only Oz docs found reference Internet Explorer and Windows shipping PCs, so the installed version matters) ([Oz Development help](https://ozdevelopment.com/help/ozlinkforups/3.6/shipping_from_netsuite.htm)); carriers and accounts; which carrier software is in use (WorldShip, FedEx Ship Manager); label printers; an export of item weights/dims (or the fact that they are blank); whether Advanced Shipping and Pick Pack Ship are enabled.

**Impact and evidence.** If the per-label time falls from a 2-minute average to about 15 seconds of scan-and-print, 500 labels take roughly 2 hours instead of 16.7, saving about **14–15 labor-hours per peak day** (arithmetic from meeting figures; the 15-second target is an assumption). Descartes' own claim is "Print labels 5X faster" (marketing) and its case studies run 400–800 packages per day ([Descartes](https://www.descartes.com/solutions/ecommerce-shipping-fulfillment/warehouse-management/descartes-ozlink-shipping-for-netsuite); [Sailrite](https://www.descartes.com/resources/download-center/video-sailrite-success-story-descartes-ozlink-mobile-high-velocity)).

**Effort.** 1–2 weeks for item data and a ShipStation pilot on one carrier; 3–4 weeks to full cutover with automation rules.

**Risks.** ShipStation's free direct connection is stated only "through the end of 2026"; confirm 2027 terms. Both ShipStation and the connector draw from the same 5-slot concurrency pool. Ship Central's 10-per-batch bulk limit in the app is a constraint at 500/day.

### Solution 5. Scan the traveler and pull the art (the bigger build; Gregg's "life-changing" ask)

**Problem.** Steve receives a paper work order, searches roughly a million Dropbox files for the right master, re-saves it in the format the destination machine needs, then shepherds the design through licensing and customer approval before production can start (meeting notes). Each step is memory- and hand-driven.

**Design principle.** Identify the job without OCR; search the library with a purpose-built index; convert deterministically; use vision models only where there is handwriting or legacy paper; track both approvals and write the result back to the work order so Solution 3 can release.

#### 5a. QR-coded traveler via the Advanced PDF template (the path that avoids OCR)

Edit the Work Order advanced PDF/HTML template (or the Advanced Manufacturing traveler template if that SuiteApp is licensed) to add `<barcode codetype="qrcode" showtext="false" height="150" width="150" value="https://art.tfg.internal/wo/${record.internalid}" />`; Oracle documents the tag, lists qrcode among supported codetypes, notes height and width are required for QR, and warns the barcode is only visible in a generated document, not the template editor ([Oracle Help](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_N2868543.html)). A QR holds up to 1,520 ASCII characters, so also encode the sales order number, line number and item SKU as a fallback for offline reading. Any phone camera opens the URL; a USB or Bluetooth 2D scanner at the art desk (estimated $100–300, consultant judgment) types it into the art-finder page. The structured fields (customer, item, school/licensor, mark type, imprint color and location, quantity, due date) are then fetched live from NetSuite via REST, so the paper is only a pointer. Shops without Advanced Manufacturing customize the Work Order PDF template to produce travelers today ([NetSuite Professionals archive](https://archive.netsuiteprofessionals.com/t/8552577/anyone-know-if-there-is-a-native-way-to-make-a-work-order-tr)); if the shop later wants shop-floor time reporting, Manufacturing Mobile runs in a browser on Android/iOS tablets and reads Code 128 and QR codes ([Anchor Group](https://www.anchorgroup.tech/blog/integrate-mobile-scm-features-netsuite)).

#### 5b. Fallback vision extraction for handwritten annotations and legacy travelers

Travelers already in the pile, handwritten CSR or artist annotations, and customer-supplied PDFs still need reading. Photograph or scan the page, send it to Claude Haiku 5.5 with a strict JSON schema whose enums are the shop's controlled vocabularies (licensor list, mark types such as primary/wordmark/mascot/Mom/Dad/Alumni, imprint locations, machine list), and ask for a per-field confidence. Cost is negligible: a rendered page is at most 1,568 image tokens, plus 1,500–3,000 text tokens if a text layer exists, and Haiku 5.5 is $0.10 per million input tokens ([Claude PDF support](https://docs.claude.com/en/docs/build-with-claude/pdf-support); [Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)), so under a tenth of a cent per traveler (arithmetic). Accuracy is the constraint: a February 2026 benchmark of seven systems on real hand-filled forms found "even the best-performing models in our benchmark struggled to consistently exceed 95% business-level accuracy on real handwritten forms," with GPT-5 Mini and Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite leading on field accuracy and Claude Sonnet "moderate, usable" ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/handwriting-vs-ai-real-performance-of-ai-on-handwritten-documents)). So the review UI is mandatory and the model is swappable: run the recommended 20–50 traveler bake-off across two or three vendors before committing. The review screen follows the practitioner pattern: traveler image on the left, extracted fields and proposed art candidates on the right, three-tier routing (auto-accept at or above 0.92, pre-filled review 0.70–0.92, manual below 0.70), with handwritten fields held to stricter thresholds and every low-confidence or master-data-mismatched field highlighted but never blanked ([lilting.ch](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/human-in-loop-document-extraction-confidence); [theneuralbase.com](https://theneuralbase.com/ocr-for-ai/learn/advanced/human-review-workflow-design/)). Deterministic validation runs beside the model: school name must resolve to a licensor in the master list, Pantone strings must be in that school's palette, SKU must exist in NetSuite.

#### 5c. The Dropbox index: build your own, do not rely on Dropbox search

Dropbox's native capabilities fall short at this scale. Its staff note that search results "are not technically exhaustive" and that above 10,000 matches you must use `list_folder` instead ([Dropbox Community](https://community.dropbox.com/en/discussion/340092/dropbox-api-search-vs-recursive-list-folder-with-filtered-results)); `search_v2` is tokenized rather than substring and lags after moves ([Dropbox Community](https://community.dropbox.com/en/discussion/765443/python-sdk-api-inconsistent-results-with-files-search-v2)); the thumbnails API only renders jpg, jpeg, png, tiff, gif and bmp under 20 MB ([Dropbox migration guide](https://www.dropbox.com/developers/reference/migration-guide)); and Dropbox Dash at $15–35 per user per month searches image content generically and excludes .ai/.eps from text-in-image search ([Dropbox Help: Dash](https://help.dropbox.com/view-edit/dropbox-dash-search-and-explore); [toolchase](https://toolchase.com/tool/dropbox-dash/)). The build has five layers. *Crawl:* call `files/list_folder` per top-level folder with `recursive=false` and breadth-first traversal (the SDK warns recursive listing of very large trees causes performance issues or errors), paginate with `continue` at up to 2,000 entries per page, and store every FileMetadata (stable `id`, path, name, size, `rev`, `server_modified`, `content_hash`) in Postgres ([Dropbox Python SDK](https://dropbox-sdk-python.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/dropbox.html); [fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/dropbox-api-list-files/)). A million files is roughly 500 pages of metadata, hours not days (arithmetic). *Parse:* a rules-plus-LLM normalizer turns folder paths and filenames (decades of them likely encode school, mark, color count, Pantones and dates) into structured attributes (licensor ID, mark type, color count, Pantone list, year, format), with the LLM filling a schema whose enums are the controlled lists. *Render:* download each vector file and render a PNG thumbnail locally with Ghostscript (`png16m`), because PDF-compatible .ai files begin with `%PDF` and render directly, .eps/.ps go through the PostScript interpreter, and files saved without "Create PDF Compatible File" show only a placeholder and should be flagged for a one-time Illustrator re-save batch ([Adobe Help](https://helpx.adobe.com/illustrator/kb/optimize-native-pdf-file-sizes.html); [Ghostscript docs](https://ghostscript.readthedocs.io/en/latest/VectorDevices.html)). Rendering a million thumbnails is the long pole: budget days of background compute and honor every `Retry-After` header, since Dropbox rate-limits per authorization and recommends batch endpoints ([DBX Performance Guide](https://developers.dropbox.com/dbx-performance-guide)). *Embed:* CLIP ViT-B/32 produces 512-dimensional vectors at roughly 20–50 ms per image on CPU; store them in pgvector with an HNSW index for sub-second nearest-neighbor search ([the-main-thread.com](https://www.the-main-thread.com/p/quarkus-onnx-pgvector-image-search-tutorial)). *Keep current:* subscribe to Dropbox webhooks or `list_folder/longpoll` rather than polling, and write the canonical attributes back as Dropbox file properties (team template, up to 32 properties) so the metadata is visible in Dropbox and survives moves ([Dropbox Python SDK](https://dropbox-sdk-python.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/dropbox.html)). Dedup collapses the million: `content_hash` finds byte-identical copies and CLIP distance finds the same mark re-saved as .ai, .pdf, .eps and .png. The query for a job is a structured filter (licensor, mark type, color count) ranked by recency and by whether a production-ready variant for the target machine already exists, with CLIP text-to-image search as the fallback when attributes are missing. Official licensor masters should be downloaded once from JPatton's Logos on Demand into a controlled `/Licensor Masters/<School>/<date>` tree and treated as authoritative in the index; CLC's terms prohibit "systematic retrieval" and no download API exists, so this stays a manual, quarterly step triggered by JPatton change emails ([CLC Terms of Use](https://clc.com/terms-of-use/); [CLC application guide](https://authorzilla.com/o0VEB/clc-license-application-retail.html)).

#### 5e. Two-stage approval tracker with Brand Manager 360 pre-fill

The tracker's data model is Design (art file id, licensor, design number, colorways, product) → LicensingApproval (agency: CLC, Affinity, in-house; portal submission id; submitted/approved/rejected dates; expiry = approval date plus one year, since schools such as Liberty require annual re-approval) → CustomerApproval (proof version, link, approved by and at) ([Liberty University](https://www.liberty.edu/marketing/licensing-branding/faq/)). For CLC schools, the client API documented on BM360's staging and QA hosts exposes `POST Artwork/SubmitArtwork` with LicenseCode, DesignNo, Colors, RetailerName, SupplierId, Upis, ArtFileName, ArtFileData, SelectedLogoApplications and SelectedProductCombinations, plus `GetAvailableLicenses`, `GetLogoApplications`, `GetSubmittedArtQueueById` and `GetArtwork` for lookups and status ([BM360 client API](https://clientapistaging.brandmanager360.com/); [SubmitArtwork](https://clientapiqa.brandmanager360.com/Help/Api/POST-Artwork-SubmitArtwork)). Those fields map almost one-to-one onto data already in NetSuite and the art index, so "one click: submit this proof to BM360 and record the queue ID" is buildable, then a poller updates status. Production API access, authentication and whether TFG's license tier qualifies are unverified and must be asked of CLC. Affinity Gateway and other portals remain manual uploads with a "mark as submitted" step ([Affinity Licensing](https://affinitylicensing.com/licensing)). Reuse logic is the quiet win: if the same design, licensor and product combination has an unexpired licensing approval, the licensing leg is skipped and only the customer leg runs. Customer e-approval is a public link rendering a templated proof (per-SKU mockup template with print-area mask, composited with Pillow or a headless browser, the same renderer that later makes per-variant storefront images), approve or request-changes buttons, typed-name signature, timestamp and reminders at 2 and 5 days; GraphicsFlow at $99/month is the stopgap if the shop wants customer e-approvals immediately ([GraphicsFlow](https://www.graphicsflow.com/)). On both approvals the tracker PATCHes the NetSuite work order's custom flags and art-file reference via REST, which is what Solution 3's release script reads.

**Buy vs build.** Build. No product found indexes a Dropbox library by licensor and mark, models licensor approval as a stage with expiry, or writes back to NetSuite; decorator MIS tools (Printavo $109–399/month, DecoNetwork ~$199/month) assume they are the order system and would duplicate NetSuite ([Printavo](https://www.printavo.com/pricing/); [Capterra DecoNetwork](https://www.capterra.com/p/186114/DecoNetwork/pricing/)). Incremental run cost is an LLM bill in the tens of dollars a month, hosting for Postgres/pgvector and the thumbnail store, and one Illustrator seat ($22.99/month) on the conversion box; Affinity is free since October 2025 if an Illustrator-free viewer is wanted ([SVGator](https://www.svgator.com/blog/what-is-an-ai-file/)).

**Data and access needed.** A Dropbox Business/team API app with team-folder scope; two or three real top-level folders and a sample of filenames to design the parser; the current traveler as a PDF and knowledge of which template produces it; the machine list with software versions; Steve's half-day to walk through how he searches today; CLC license code(s) and a request to CLC for BM360 API credentials; the current customer-proofing process; approval volumes per week and rejection reasons.

**Impact and evidence.** The identification step collapses to a scan; the search step collapses from a hunt to picking among ranked thumbnails, which for indexed and classified files should be under a minute (inference; proxies are 10–15 minutes per DAM search and 5–15 minute job budgets cited above); format conversion becomes zero-touch for the automatable rows above; licensing resubmission is avoided whenever an approval is still valid. ASI's October 2026 webinar reported a promo distributor whose vibe-coded agent "has saved his staff 25% of their time," the nearest industry analog ([ASI Central](https://members.asicentral.com/news/strategy/october-2026/how-3-promo-distributors-are-embracing-ai/)). No published case measures AI art retrieval in a decorator, which is why the two-week baseline measurement is part of the plan.

**Effort.** 10–14 weeks to a production v1 in stages: QR traveler and art-finder shell (2 weeks), crawl and index with thumbnails (3–4 weeks of mostly background compute), parser and ranked search (2 weeks), conversion service and hot folders (2 weeks), approval tracker with BM360 and NetSuite write-back (3 weeks), vision fallback and review UI (1–2 weeks, overlapping).

**Risks.** Filename conventions may be inconsistent enough that the parser covers less than the hoped 60–90% of files; CLIP has no published benchmark on collegiate marks and must be validated on the shop's thumbnails; non-PDF-compatible .ai files need a re-save batch; BM360 production API access is unconfirmed; CLC's "systematic retrieval" clause must be respected for Logos on Demand. A generative model must never draw or alter a mark; commercial use of an AI-generated image resembling a registered mark is infringing "even if they acted without intent or awareness" ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/uk/trademark/1656760/from-pixels-to-problems-ai-image-generators-and-trade-mark-risks)).

### Solution 6. Emailed wholesale PO intake into NetSuite (medium build, after Solution 1)

**Problem.** Small-account and wholesale orders arriving as PDFs, spreadsheets and emails are re-keyed (meeting notes; the pre-meeting research put the industry figure at roughly seven in ten B2B orders re-keyed at $30–$100 each) ([Workd/Conexiom](https://www.workd.com/insights/articles/hidden-cost-managing-b2b-orders-email-2026/)).

**Technical design.** Mailbox ingestion → Claude Sonnet 5.5 vision extraction with a strict JSON schema (ship-to, PO number, lines with school, product, mark, color, quantity, price, requested date) → fuzzy SKU match against NetSuite item records with customer-specific aliases → side-by-side review UI reusing Solution 5's component → POST a NetSuite sales order via REST with `orderStatus` set by Solution 2's rules → acknowledgement email draft. Boldare's Claude Vision PO pipeline reports 15 minutes per PO falling to seconds and error rates from 5% to under 0.5%; flux8labs went from 60% to over 98% after SKU-matching work ([Boldare](https://www.boldare.com/blog/automated-purchase-order-processing-claude-vision-databricks/); [flux8labs](https://dev.to/flux8labs/how-we-automated-purchase-orders-from-gmail-to-tally-using-gpt-4-98-extraction-accuracy-ceg)). Run cost is cents per PO.

**Buy vs build.** Build; Conexiom and Esker are enterprise-priced and Rossum is the only packaged alternative at this size ([Elentaria](https://elentaria.ai/compare/conexiom-vs-esker)). **Data needed:** a month of real POs, the customer alias list, Follett/BNC vendor rules. **Effort:** 3–6 weeks. **Risks:** PII in prompts; SKU aliasing is most of the work. Tag: bigger build, phase 2.

### Solution 7. A NetSuite MCP exception agent for Gregg (medium build, low cost)

**Problem.** Gregg's control today is exercised by clicking through every record. Once Solutions 2–5 remove the clicks, he still needs a daily view of what is stuck and why, without opening a dozen saved searches.

**Technical design.** NetSuite's AI Connector Service is "not a paid feature and the MCP Standard Tools is a free SuiteApp"; the endpoint is `https://<accountid>.suitetalk.api.netsuite.com/services/mcp/v1/all`, it requires a non-Administrator role with "MCP Server Connection" and "OAuth 2.0 Access Tokens" permissions, exposes record CRUD, record actions, saved searches, reports and read-only SuiteQL, and lets developers add SuiteScript 2.1 `@NScriptType CustomTool` scripts ([Oracle Help: AI Connector FAQ](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/article_4160616848.html); [Custom Tool Script Type](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/article_1185045525.html)). Claude Pro and Claude Code are documented clients. Build three or four custom tools with a narrow role: `list_blocked_work_orders` (Planned WOs grouped by missing licensing approval, missing customer approval, component shortage), `list_held_sales_orders` (Pending Approval with the failing rule), `release_work_orders(ids)` (gated to the same rule as the Map/Reduce) and `fulfillments_missing_weight`. Deterministic scripts do the high-volume work; the agent is for Gregg's morning triage and ad hoc questions. Tools "can't run as administrators, can't call external APIs and can't run elevated scripts" and share the concurrency pool ([Oracle Help](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/article_4160616848.html)).

**Buy vs build.** Build on the free first-party service rather than a third-party NetSuite MCP, because Oracle's tools inherit role permissions and audit logging (execution logs retained 21 days). **Data needed:** a custom role, Claude Pro seats. **Effort:** 1–2 weeks after Solution 3's fields exist. **Risks:** 2026.2 MCP Apps were "planned" per Oracle's July 2026 release, so interactive UIs inside the client may not be available yet ([StreetInsider PR](https://www.streetinsider.com/PRNewswire/NetSuite+Extends+Commitment+to+Helping+Businesses+Use+AI+Their+Way/26243590.html)). Tag: medium build.

## Addendum: what the shop-floor photos change (added 18 Nov 2026, after the debrief)

Eight photos from the facility (travelers, the print workstation, the machines, NetSuite on a floor laptop; observations recorded in `research_notes/Fanatic Group AI opportunities/shop_floor_photos_2026-11-18.md`) resolve several of the discovery questions above and simplify the biggest build.

**The traveler already carries the two things the art finder needs.** Every NetSuite Work Order printout has a 1D barcode with the work order number at the top right, and it prints a **Frontside File Path** field such as `TFG Team Folder/CUSTOM ORDERS/FANATICS/DROP SHIP/KENT STATE/RSST2/KES-FSB-PRIM-WDMK-15.eps`, plus columns for Backside File Name and Backside File Path and a product thumbnail. The Dropbox path of the art is therefore already a field on the NetSuite assembly item. Today a press operator reads that path off the paper, types it into the Dropbox desktop app beside the RIP, opens the .eps, and builds the print job by hand; the photos show exactly that, with the Dropbox panel listing `XU-FAN-CM102-GRAY.eps` and `TUL-FAN-CM102-GREEN.eps` viewed minutes apart and a stack of travelers on the keyboard tray. Solution 5 collapses accordingly:

- **5a becomes "scan the existing barcode," not "add a QR."** A $100–300 USB barcode scanner at each press reads the WO number; a small web app looks the work order up in NetSuite (REST or SuiteQL), reads the frontside and backside path fields, fetches the files from Dropbox through the API, and opens them or drops them into the RIP's input folder named by WO number. Adding a QR to the template remains a nice-to-have for phone cameras but is no longer on the critical path.
- **Better still, stage the art before anyone scans.** A scheduled job can run against every work order that reaches Released (Solution 3), resolve its path fields, fetch and convert the files, and write them into a per-day, per-machine queue folder (`/PrintQueue/2026-11-19/X5/WO475071_KES-RH-RSST2-15.eps`). The operator then never searches at all. This is the version that matches Gregg's "pull the art file itself."
- **5b (vision extraction) drops to a true edge case**: customer-supplied PDFs and handwritten notes only.
- **5c (the Dropbox index) is still worth building, but as validation and fallback rather than primary lookup.** The photos show why: the Kent State assembly `KES-RH-RSST2-15` points to a file named with an FSB bottle prefix inside the RSST2 folder, so path fields can be stale or reused across item types. The index should verify that the referenced file exists, flag mismatches between item code and filename, and propose candidates when the field is empty or the backside is missing. The filename convention is parseable (`SCHOOL-PROGRAM-ITEM-DESIGN-COLOR/VARIANT`, e.g. `AZ-FAN-RSST2-15-ALUM`, `BNC-CM102-WDMK-MSCT-13`), which makes the parser in 5c straightforward.
- **5d is now anchored to real machines.** An **Inkcups X5** UV flatbed is confirmed, driven by **ColorPRINT Server 5.0** as the RIP ("X-Jet S1 ink CMYKWW 1200x600", spot-white layer) and a PCS 6 printer-control panel; a second UV unit with a generic controller sits beside it (likely a Helix-class rotary for tumblers, to confirm). Masters are .eps; the RIP saves .prt job files. The conversion matrix should be re-cut around ColorPRINT's accepted inputs and hot-folder behaviour, which is the next thing to ask Gregg or Inkcups support.

**A second automation target appears: nesting.** The RIP queue shows hand-built jobs named `Nest11-18_1` and `Nest11-18_2` that gang several work orders' marks onto one bed, alongside single-WO jobs. Grouping released work orders by machine, blank item and colorway (for example all `RSST2-NAVY` jobs for the X5 that day) and pre-building the nest is a natural extension of the staging job above and directly raises press throughput. Whether ColorPRINT accepts job tickets or hot-folder nesting is a discovery item.

**The B2B channel is more automated than the meeting notes implied.** Both photographed travelers were created from sales orders numbered `EDI2531xx` for customers `11957-059 Kansas Sampler Corporate : Kansas Sampler - Store 059 - Fairview Park` and `Store 273 - Gilbert Gateway`. Kansas Sampler Enterprises is the parent of Rally House, each store is a sub-customer, and each work order is 12 units to one store. So Rally House replenishment already flows in by EDI, and the manual re-keying Gregg described is confined to web, TikTok and small accounts, which narrows Solution 1 to exactly the Shopify connector. It also explains the label volume: 500 shipments a day are store-level drop shipments of 12-unit work orders. Two questions follow for Solution 4: does Rally House require GS1-128 carton labels and EDI 856 advance ship notices, and if so does the EDI provider or OzLINK produce them today?

**Royalty data is already structured.** Each traveler prints a Royalty Code (KES, ARZ) and Royalty Agency ("70600 CLC College Licensing", "1000007 Affinity Licensed Product"), so item-level royalty attribution exists in NetSuite. Solution 8 from the pre-meeting report (the royalty engine) becomes a SuiteQL report over item fulfillments joined to those fields, not a new data-capture project.

**Smaller confirmations.** NetSuite runs in a browser on an HP laptop on a wire shelf by the shipping bench; a UPS-branded mousepad sits on that bench, consistent with the WorldShip-plus-OzLINK pattern; a wall chart maps 22 product colors to numbers; and a handwritten list of affinity variants (Generic, Pricing Barcode, Alumni, Grandma, Grandpa, Mom, Dad) sits next to the NetSuite laptop, which is the controlled vocabulary the art index should use.

**Revised 90-day effect.** The art-finder MVP moves earlier and shrinks: barcode scanner, NetSuite lookup, Dropbox fetch, open-in-RIP can be demonstrated within the first two-week window after access is granted, with the staging job and nest builder following once Solution 3 produces a clean Released queue. The discovery checklist gains four items: the ColorPRINT version and its hot-folder/job-ticket options; the second machine's model; the EDI provider and Rally House label/ASN requirements; and a sample export of the item records' file-path fields to measure how many are empty or stale.

Web and TikTok order volumes, work orders per day, days per year at peak and the art-desk minutes per job were not quantified in the meeting and are the first things the baseline must measure.

## Conclusion

The meeting turned a storefront engagement into a throughput engagement, and the research says most of the throughput problem is already solved inside products Gregg pays for. Three of the four gates fall to a preference, a short script, a $200-a-month connector and a shipping integration that is free through year-end; the fourth, the art path, is the one place a custom AI build earns its keep, and even there the design that works best is the one that uses the model least: a QR code that makes the paper a pointer, a database that makes the million files searchable, deterministic converters that make the machine formats zero-touch, and a vision model held in reserve for handwriting. The earlier report's instinct that "the language model was roughly ten percent of the work" ([nikhil.pro](https://nikhil.pro/ai-workflows-for-shopify/)) applies here with unusual force.

The strategic implication is that the floor work and the Owala work are the same project in two phases. Every demand-side idea from the pre-meeting report pours more orders into a pipe whose capacity is one man's clicks and two people's typing; remove those, and the connector, the mockup renderer and the licensor master data built for the floor become the foundation the storefront program needs anyway. The 90-day plan should be judged by four numbers Gregg can read off his own system in February: orders keyed by hand (target zero), work orders Gregg released by hand (target exceptions only), minutes per label (target under a quarter of today's), and minutes from traveler to correct master file at Steve's desk (target under one).
